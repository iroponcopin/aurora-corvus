Changelog

The update history of OUKA, Cherry and Aureum, and the notice that ended Alpha. Choose a brand to see what changed in each release, newest first.

Updated 2026-09-29

The damage, speed, and other numbers shown here are the default values at the time of that release. Server-side config adjustments may mean the numbers you actually experience in-game are different.

No updates match your filters.

New feature

Thirty pieces for a Japanese room, and ten facilities in the second industrial update. Tatami and engawa, shoji and fusuma, ranma and byobu, a chabudai and a tansu, a kakejiku, an incense burner, lanterns, a shishi-odoshi, a furin and a go board. For the factory: a transceiver that sends items through walls, a miner that digs with your tool, a bus that draws straight from the vault, and more. Every new sound is synthesised from scratch.

Highlights (10)

  • Tatami: an igusa mat covers 2 blocks with one of 4 borders, and standing in a room of 8 or more joined cells makes hunger drain half as fast. Borderless checkered tatami laid together form a checkerboard.
  • Shoji and fusuma: an empty hand or a redstone signal slides them about one block open, and two side by side slide past each other. Four fusuma in a row make one gold-leaf landscape.
  • Kamoi and nageshi lintels: a carved ranma fits on top, and a kakejiku, a sword stand, a noren, a sudare or a furin hangs from them. The noren parts on the side you walk through; up to 3 sudare roll as one.
  • Furniture: sit on a zabuton or a zaisu, set out up to 4 things on a chabudai, and keep 27 stacks in a tansu. Whisk and drink matcha for full health and Haste II for 3 minutes (one bowl every 5 minutes).
  • Tokonoma and garden: the kakejiku's painting follows the seasons, a new one every 8 in-game days, and the yukimi-toro gathers snow in winter. The shishi-odoshi strikes its stone every 4.4 seconds; the furin rings in the wind.
  • The OUKA Matter Transceiver: link it with a Resonance Linker and it sends up to a stack and a bucket every tick, through walls, up to 32 blocks away, from one sender to as many as four receivers. No power needed.
  • The Resonant Miner digs with the pickaxe or shovel you give it and, at normal speed, takes as long as that tool would take you. Silk Touch and Fortune work too. An area of up to 5×5, up to 16 deep.
  • The Quantum Vault I/O Bus draws the items you choose (up to 9 kinds) from the vault, up to 256 a tick, and leaves the count you set inside. An OUKA conveyor takes only 1 a tick.
  • The Hyper-Impulse Hopper moves 64 items a tick into a chest or pipe (1 onto an OUKA conveyor). The Cobblestone Synthesizer never uses up the bucket of lava and the bucket of water you give it, and, on grid power, makes cobblestone, gravel, sand or ore sand.
  • Also: a 3×3 crafter, a pump and pipes that move 8 buckets of fluid a tick, a grinder that stores experience in capsules, a terminal that opens the vault even from another dimension, and an accelerator pylon that speeds OUKA machines up to ×10.

Warnings (3)

  • This release fixes two sounds that never played in V1.0.1, V1.1.0 or V1.2.0: the OUKA Beacon terminal's slider tick and the Ouka Caster selector's confirm sound. If you are on an earlier version, please replace it.
  • You can also install it from Corvus Store: the first time, press Get in Corvus Store; after that, Corvus Store replaces it with newer versions by itself. If you have turned Automatic Updates off, press Update instead. (To install by hand, use the OUKA page.)
  • Requires Minecraft 26.3, Fabric Loader 0.19.5 or newer, Fabric API 0.161.0+26.3 or newer, GeckoLib 5.5.6 (bundled in the zip) and Java 25.

New feature

Cherry's biggest update: five next-generation guns with first-person animation and recoil, three kinds of life on the Moon, the boss Lunaris Prime sealed in a ruin far out on the Moon, the red planet Mars with its cold and iron dust storms, a navigation console that flies the Apex-1 to any of three worlds, and the Advanced Cherry Fabricator, which makes every gun, magazine and vehicle part.

Highlights (8)

  • Five guns: the CR-14 bullpup rifle, the CP-9 sub-machine gun, the CS-50 rail sniper, the CX-12 magpulse shotgun and the C-Pistol. Right-click aims and softly blurs what lies nearer and farther than your aim, R reloads (in survival with one of that gun's magazines) and F inspects; with recoil, muzzle flash, casings, holographic sights, the CS-50's 6× thermal scope and the C-Pistol's laser sight.
  • Life on the Moon: the Selenite Stalker hides under the regolith, leaps at anyone who walks without sneaking and bites holes in pressure suits; the Void Drifter's EMP blanks your guns' sights and ammo readouts; the Regolith Devourer slams the ground to fling you high into the sky and hurls molten meteors.
  • Lunaris Prime: beyond 8,000 blocks on the Moon a giant crater holds the Astraea Shackle (/locate finds it). An Astral Catalyst in its seal console wakes the boss; the sky turns crimson, and at half health its armour flies off for a second, faster phase.
  • Its relics: the Coreshard of Lunaris, the Graviton Siphon (its shot opens a black hole) and the Crown of Selene.
  • Mars: press launch on the pad to open the Apex-1's Stellar Navigation console and pick Earth, the Moon or Mars. Mars has 0.38 g and unbreathable air at -60 C: the pressure suit keeps the air out, and a Thermal Lining sewn in at the smithing table keeps the cold out.
  • Iron dust storms blow over Mars about every 20 minutes: the fog closes to a few blocks and noise crosses Cherry's HUDs.
  • Mars's resources: Martian Ferrite and Perchlorate Energy Salt. Perchlorate Propellant is rocket fuel worth three canisters.
  • The Advanced Cherry Fabricator: pick a recipe and fill its six inputs; it makes the guns, their magazines, gun parts, fuel and every vehicle, building the Apex-1 from boosters and the Dreadnought from railgun barrels that it mass-produces from helium-3 and regolith cores; hoppers can feed it.

Warnings (1)

  • Requires Minecraft 26.3, Fabric Loader 0.19.5 or newer, Fabric API 0.161.0 or newer, GeckoLib 5.5.6 or newer and Java 25.

New feature

This is the last Alpha release. It switches Alpha off. The 13 mods are replaced by placeholders that add nothing to the game, and nothing new has been added. Blocks that Alpha added turn into empty air, and once a world has been saved that way they do not come back. Back up your worlds before you update.

Highlights (3)

  • Alpha is switched off. The 13 mods are replaced by placeholders that add nothing to the game (Cherry, OUKA and Aureum are not touched)
  • Only the overworld's height limit (Y up to 1023) stays, so that nothing you built above Y=319 with ordinary blocks is deleted
  • Corvus removes the old jars, places the placeholders and keeps a backup of the files it replaced. If you installed by hand, or run a server, delete the old jars as the pack's README says

Warnings (3)

  • Back up your worlds first. Blocks that Alpha added turn into empty air when a world is opened without Alpha, and once it has been saved that way they are gone for good. Putting Alpha back does not restore them (checked on a dedicated server). Alpha's items and creatures are expected to go the same way (not tested)
  • A server and its players must move together: a player who still has Alpha cannot join a server that no longer does, and the other way round
  • If "Install updates automatically" is on in Corvus, this update is applied without asking. Corvus's backup covers the mods folder, not your worlds

Not yet implemented/confirmed at this point (1)

  • Everything else Alpha had on this site has been removed: every earlier download, its recipes, and its install guide, gates, features, roadmap and known-issues pages. This entry is all that remains of its changelog.

New feature

Twenty pieces of heavy industry: a power grid and power stations, a vast vault, conveyor and drone logistics, smelting and processing, mining and automatic harvesting, and field tools with a hazard exoskeleton. No machine is a box where numbers go up: arms, hammers, drills and rotors really move, and each machine sounds as hard as it works. This release also fixes V1.1.0's two defects.

Highlights (10)

  • A power grid: Tesla pylons link without wires within 64 blocks, and arcs run from them to the machines. No cables.
  • Power stations and storage: a fission core (up to 2 MW; it scrams at 800 °C and never explodes), a solar tower that keeps running at night on stored heat, and a 400 MJ superconducting battery.
  • The Quantum-Compression Vault: build the 5×5×7 shell and it holds up to 2,147,483,647 items, searchable by name or by "@mod".
  • Maglev conveyors carry 20 items a second. Their shape follows their neighbours, you see every item riding them, nothing falls off, and a blocked line queues. Plus a splitter and a six-way sorter.
  • Logistics drones: link two ports with a route card and a drone ferries loads between them — even through unloaded areas, arriving after the flight time.
  • Smelting and processing: an arc foundry that smelts 32 ores at sixteen times furnace speed, a hydraulic crusher for double dusts and plates, an assembly line whose three arms weld, and a centrifuge that draws out titanium, lithium and helium-3.
  • Mining and harvesting: a bore that digs ore and crude oil without end and without breaking bedrock, a desalinator that pumps 200 buckets a second, a timber mill that fells, planks and replants, and a culture vat.
  • The Omni-Wrench (turn machines round, open and close exits, overclock up to ×2.50) and the Demolition Laser (a machine and everything in it back in your inventory in one second).
  • The Holographic Blueprint: record up to 16 blocks a side, check the life-size hologram, then build it from the items you carry.
  • The Hazard Exoskeleton: shocks, heat and falls no longer hurt, you can swim in lava, and you mine three times as fast.

Warnings (3)

  • This release fixes V1.1.0's two defects (a mined Block of Ouka Alloy dropped nothing; the shelter's loot table). If you are on V1.1.0, please replace it.
  • You can also install it from Corvus: the first time, press Download in Corvus; after that, Corvus replaces it with newer versions. (To install by hand, use the OUKA page.)
  • Requires Minecraft 26.3, Fabric Loader 0.19.5 or newer, Fabric API 0.161.0+26.3 or newer, GeckoLib 5.5.6 (bundled in the zip) and Java 25.

In 1.1.0 to 1.1.3, Cherry's aircraft engines, afterburner, missile motors and lock tones, and the pressure suit's breathing, then stayed silent until you boarded again or put the helmet back on. They now start again by themselves.

Highlights (2)

  • On the Moon, the pressure suit's breathing does the same.

Warnings (1)

New feature

Flying and sailing are rebuilt so that anyone can do it. The view is no longer locked: the aircraft fly where you look, and the ships answer to W, A, S and D like a boat. The Dreadnought's railgun now strikes what it hits; in 1.1.0 to 1.1.2 its shells damaged nothing.

Highlights (11)

  • Aircraft fly where you look: turn your head and the Stratos-900 or the Aegis-X banks, turns, climbs or dives towards it by herself. W and S set the throttle; left-click fires the Aegis-X's missiles at the target the radar picks by itself.
  • Flaps, landing gear, brakes, reverse thrust, the afterburner, the canopy and the arresting hook now work by themselves, and a short guide on the screen shows the controls when you board.
  • The view is yours: it no longer locks to first person or tilts with the aircraft, F5 works in flight, and holding Left Alt lets you look around while she keeps her course.
  • Ships: standing at a station shows the key that takes it. At the wheel, W and S drive the engine (astern below zero), A and D steer, and letting go centres the wheel and holds her heading; Sneak leaves. The ferry's doors are on Y.
  • With nobody at the gunnery console, the Dreadnought's helmsman fires the guns from the wheel: left-click for a salvo, right-click for the VLS.
  • Fixed from 1.1.2: the rudder turned the ship the opposite way to its keys, astern gave no thrust, a ship could not be launched from the shore (she now floats with her stern just past the water you aim at), and a ship could not be picked back up in survival.
  • The railgun's shells burst on the first block, creature, ship or aircraft in their path and damage what lies within 4 m; when a salvo lands on one target, all six shells count. Look at any part of a hull and both turrets train on that point.
  • The gun spares the ship that fired it and her crew, and it never breaks blocks. No ship or aircraft is sunk or brought down by a single salvo, and manning the guns no longer loads distant chunks.
  • A death message from one of Cherry's weapons no longer shows an untranslated key when the attacker holds a renamed item.
  • The Aegis-X's missile count at the bottom left of the flight display is no longer cut off by the bottom of the screen. With the automatic GUI scale this happened at many screen sizes, among them 720p, 1440p, 4K and the game's default window.
  • Chart waypoints are accepted only within the chart's widest view, about 1.4 km from the ship, which also protects servers from malformed waypoint requests.

Warnings (3)

  • The controls have changed: 1.1.2's flight keys (R, U, Y, K, Z, N, M, J and the arrow keys) and helm keys (, . = - ; ') are gone, and any of them you rebound are no longer used.
  • Railgun shells now hurt whatever they strike, including other players' ships and aircraft. The crew member directing the guns is credited with the damage.
  • Requires Minecraft 26.3, Fabric Loader 0.19.5 or newer, Fabric API 0.161.0 or newer, GeckoLib 5.5.6 or newer and Java 25.

New feature

The Leviathan and the Dreadnought get their real models and interiors, and every station aboard now works from the game. In 1.1.0 and 1.1.1 the railgun, the VLS and the chart table could not actually be used; now they can.

Highlights (6)

  • Real ships: hulls, decks and interiors are fully modelled — the ferry's vehicle deck, lounge, observation deck and bridge, and the destroyer's bridge, CIC and hangar — with windows you can see through that light up at night. Every deck you see is a deck you can walk on.

Both train on the point you look at and fire one salvo on a left click, with muzzle flashes, blast rings, tracers and recoiling barrels. A right click fires the VLS at the radar-locked target once its hatch is open, venting white smoke.
  • The Leviathan's bow visor and bow and stern ramps: stopped at the wheel, press the doors key (' by default). The visor rises before the ramp lowers, the engine stays locked until everything is shut again, and you can walk up the lowered ramps.
  • Wake and bubbles: a ship under way throws a bow wave at the Kelvin angle and leaves a wake behind her, and a turning screw churns bubbles.
  • The helm keys no longer clash with Minecraft's own keys: , and . turn the wheel, = and - set the engine, and ; holds the heading. The helm instruments now sit above the hotbar and health bar instead of on top of them.

Warnings (2)

Highlights (10)

  • A 328-block pedestal of netherite, diamond, emerald or Ouka alloy in eight layers. It wakes only when all 328 are there.
  • A 1,000-block aura that reaches players even while the beacon's own area is unloaded. Spectators receive nothing.
  • Ten effects tuned steplessly from 0.00 to 20.00: speed, mining, attack speed, strength, jump, safe fall, absorption, regeneration, food, resistance and night vision.

New feature

A separate optimisation mod for Minecraft 26.2 (Fabric), apart from Alpha. It works on game logic, memory and the server tick, never touches chunk rendering, and so runs beside Sodium and the Alpha pack. For higher FPS, install Sodium.

Highlights (4)

  • Shared block-state caches and a lighter thread-contention detector: 6.3 MB less resident heap with 2,209 chunks loaded (two independent measurements agree).
  • An expiry time for the structure template cache: 17.2 MB less heap while generating new land (two measurements agree). Unsaved templates in a structure block are never dropped.
  • Each optimisation can be switched off on its own in config/aureum.json, and a switched-off item leaves vanilla code untouched.
  • /aureum (needs permission) shows what is enabled and how much is being shared.

Warnings (3)

  • Tick time (MSPT) is unchanged within measurement noise; Aureum makes no speed claim.
  • The two client-side settings (a particle limit and a block-entity render distance cap) are off by default, and their effect has not been measured.
  • Requires Fabric Loader 0.19.3 or later, Fabric API, and Java 25 or later.

New feature

Corvus Store 3.0.1 is a small release with two changes.

The Updates page now works the way the App Store's does. It has no "Check for Updates" button and no line about when it last checked. It checks by itself when you open it (unless it checked a moment ago), every fifteen minutes while Corvus Store is open, and when you press Cmd+R (Ctrl+R on Windows and Linux). When two or more updates are waiting, an "Update All" button (すべてアップデート in Japanese) appears at the right end of the page's title and updates them one after another, Corvus Store itself last; with only one waiting there is no such button. It never starts a first install, it does not try an update twice, and if something else is writing into your mods folder it stops instead of refusing card by card.

The picture beside your name was a black disc in 3.0.0 — in the sidebar and at the top of the account sheet, for every account picture and every monogram. The ring that outlines the picture had lost its style when the old settings panels were removed, and a circle is filled black unless told otherwise. It is now a hairline and nothing else, and a picture with see-through pixels sits on the card's grey. That was a fault of 3.0.0, and nothing you did.

The official icons of OUKA, Cherry, Aureum, Tsubomi, Noctua and ASTRAEA, which came with 3.0.0, are unchanged. Everything else is 3.0.0.

Verified: 913 unit tests pass on each of two clean builds, with the compiler set to refuse any warning, and the two builds made byte-identical programs. The Store was drawn without a window in all 13 languages (138 checks each, English, Japanese and Arabic included), including a check that reads the account picture pixel by pixel — with the 3.0.0 code it reads black, and with this one it reads the picture — and the Updates page: no check button, one check when it is entered, Update All appearing at two waiting updates and gone at one, and going through them in turn with Corvus Store last. It was run against the real update check with a manifest served from this machine (38 checks). The built application was driven in a real window twice: all 94 of the window instrument's claims held, none failed, none were undecided, including that Cmd+R starts one check and that opening Updates after a while starts one. Perch's own instrument, on this build: 47 claims held in each of two runs, none failed, and three in each could not be decided because the machine was busy (Perch is unchanged since 3.0.0). The macOS installer, built by CI, was opened here and the app inside it run: it reports 3.0.1, opens its window and draws all five screens, with no check button on Updates and the account picture showing; it is built for Apple Silicon Macs only. Not verified, and worth saying: the fifteen-minute wait itself (the decision is tested, the timer is the system's); an Update All run against real downloads (the order and the stopping are tested with stand-ins; the installs it presses are the same ones GET and UPDATE press); a slow check is invisible, because the cards keep showing what they showed while one runs; Corvus Store replacing itself on an installed copy; the Windows and Linux installers, which nobody has installed or run; and the words "Update All" in the twelve languages other than Japanese, which nobody who speaks them has read.

New feature

Corvus is now Corvus Store: a store front for the studio's products, laid out the way the App Store is. A sidebar down the left has Discover, Mods, Library and Updates, and your computer account at the foot; a search field at the top searches the products by name (蕾, ノクチュア and アストレア find Tsubomi, Noctua and ASTRAEA) and by what they are. Discover is a column of big cards — the newest releases first, then every product — and pressing a card grows it into its full article; pull the article down, press Escape or press the cross and it shrinks back into the card it came from. Each product has its own page: icon, developer, the version, what it runs on, its languages, a description, What's New with a Version History of every release, and a table of facts. A number that is not published is shown as a dash, never guessed.

Pressing your account at the foot of the sidebar opens the one place where anything is set: Automatic Updates, the Minecraft folder mods are installed into, Language, Perch, Solo Play Tuning, and Export Diagnostic Logs, which saves what Corvus Store has written about itself to a file. There is no settings window and no log viewer. Automatic Updates is on unless you turn it off: with it on, Corvus Store keeps itself and the mods it installed up to date; with it off, nothing updates by itself. Alpha, which has ended, is no longer updated by Corvus Store at all — its last release turns the blocks it added into air, so nothing applies it for you.

The button on every card is the App Store's: GET becomes a ring with a stop square in it while the package downloads, with the speed and the time left under it, then OPEN — which opens the official Minecraft Launcher — and UPDATE when a newer version is out. Stopping a download deletes the partial file and leaves your mods folder exactly as it was. OUKA, Cherry and Aureum can be installed and updated from a card; Tsubomi, Noctua and ASTRAEA say "Coming soon"; Alpha, which has ended, shows the site's own notice and no download.

The window is flat: a black canvas, solid dark-grey cards with a hairline edge, blue for the one thing to press. The glass of 2.0 to 2.3 is gone, and on macOS only the sidebar lets the system's own translucent material show through.

The L Window is now Perch, and it is a different thing: a solid black capsule with continuous corners that floats just below the menu bar and needs no notch. It keeps out of sight while the app that is playing — Spotify or Music — is in front, and pops out with a spring the moment you leave it; tap it, or rest the pointer on it, and it opens into a control deck with the cover, title, volume, output and the transport buttons. Your hearts, and whether you had it switched on, carry over from the L Window. Perch is macOS only in this version.

The app, its installers and its update address are still called Corvus, so every installed copy can update itself from the same place.

Verified: 899 unit tests pass on every one of three clean builds, with the compiler set to refuse any warning. The Store was drawn without a window in all 13 languages (118 checks each, English, Japanese and Arabic included) and run against the real update check with a manifest served from this machine (38 checks). The built application was driven in a real window twice: all 92 of the window instrument's claims held, none failed, none were undecided. Perch's own instrument, on the finished build: 46 or 47 claims held in each of five runs, none failed, and three in each could not be decided because the machine was busy (an earlier build of the same Perch code held 49 twice); one run in which the pointer of the person using the machine sat over the capsule is not counted. The macOS installer, built by CI, was opened here and the app inside it run: it reports 3.0.0, opens its window and draws all five screens (Discover, Mods, Library, Updates and Search). It is built for Apple Silicon Macs only. Not verified, and worth saying: Corvus Store replacing itself on an installed copy (the download, the checksum and the refusals are tested; the swap was not run); nobody has seen the sidebar's system material on a screen, felt Perch under a trackpad, seen it on a display with a notch, or pressed GET on a card for a first install; the Windows and Linux installers have not been installed or run by anyone; and the wording of the new badges, subtitles and the Perch switch in the twelve languages other than English (Japanese: Tsubomi's line is the studio's own) is a translation that nobody who speaks them has read.

New feature

Every animation in Corvus is now a damped spring rather than a fixed duration, and they are all stepped on one clock that stops when nothing is moving. A motion you interrupt keeps the speed it already had instead of starting again, so nothing jumps under your hand. Buttons take 0.95 under the pointer and light up; the switch is jelly; the sliders are liquid; the glass is built in layers — a blurred, saturated backdrop, a veil, a lit edge and a highlight that follows the pointer.

Dialogs and Solo Play Tuning can now be thrown away. Each carries a grab bar, follows the pointer when pulled down and resists on a rubber band pulled the other way. What happens when you let go is decided by how fast you were moving over the last tenth of a second, not by how far the sheet has travelled: thrown down hard it leaves at the speed you threw it, let go gently it goes only if the momentum it still carries would carry it past two fifths of its own height, and otherwise it springs back. The window behind it stands back while it is up and comes forward again in step with your finger, so changing your mind costs nothing.

On macOS a capsule now hangs from the top of the screen, above the menu bar, on every Space, and lets every click that is not on it pass straight through. It has four shapes and morphs between them: the notch alone, a minimal capsule with the cover and a dancing waveform, a Now Bar with the title, artist and the controls, and a console with shuffle, repeat, a volume bar and your output device. A tap opens it, a long press or a downward swipe opens the console, and a swipe up, Escape or a click elsewhere folds it away. A new track surfaces the Now Bar for four seconds and it tucks itself back — unless your pointer is resting on it. It reads what Music and Spotify broadcast about themselves, which needs no account and no permission of any kind; Corvus does not sign in to anything. A switch on General ("Show the L Window") turns it off, and it is on to begin with.

OUKA and ASTRAEA join Cherry on Update: each has its own row, the first install is a button you press, and after that new versions install themselves. A package that carries a jar the manifest does not list is refused whole, and a dependency two brands share — GeckoLib — belongs to neither of them, so installing one never removes or downgrades the other's copy.

The changelog was showing its own mark-up. The history is written for the website, which renders it; Corvus was drawing it as plain text, so 206 lines of the published English history, spread across 15 of its entries, were showing tags around the words they were meant to emphasise. Those lines are now laid out properly, with the bold parts actually bold, and only the two marks the feed really uses are treated as mark-up — the entries document commands with angle brackets in them, and treating those as tags would have deleted the argument names from the instructions.

Verified: 729 unit tests pass twice, and two instruments drive the built application and check what it does rather than what it was asked to do — 80 claims on the window (including a stress test that lets nothing finish and then asks whether every value is still a number, still in range and exactly at rest) and 40 on the capsule. Both ran twice against the jar published here, with the display kept awake, and nothing failed in any of the four runs. A handful of claims came back as "cannot say" rather than as a pass: this machine was busy, and where a claim needs a quiet one the instrument refuses to judge it instead of calling it a pass. The load is written into the line. Not verified, and worth saying: nobody has thrown a sheet with a trackpad or seen the capsule's frosted backdrop on a screen, the media controls have never been sent to a real player, the volume and output device have never actually been set, there is no notched screen or Intel Mac here, and one thing measured and left unfixed — the moment a sheet arrives or leaves is drawn at 60 frames a second rather than 120, which predates this version and is the crossfade behind it, not the sheet. The macOS, Windows and Linux installers are built by CI on each of those systems. The macOS one was opened here and the app inside it run: it reports 2.3.0, opens its window and draws all four screens. The Windows and Linux ones have not been installed or run by anyone.

New feature

The changelog can now show OUKA, Cherry, Alpha or Aureum. A row of brand buttons sits above it: choose one to read that brand's history, newest first, and "Open on the website" opens the same brand's page. OUKA shows its message for now. Corvus reads the list of brands from the website; if the list is missing or cannot be read, the changelog shows Alpha's history exactly as before and the reason goes to the Log. A brand whose history fails to load shows the failure and a Retry button, and the other brands still work.

Verified: all 587 tests pass, twice, including checks that the list of brands is read strictly and that a broken list falls back to Alpha. This version was photographed reading the website's new files from a local copy, once with each brand chosen first. Not yet seen: switching between brands by clicking in the running app, and the macOS, Windows and Linux installers, which are built by CI on each of those systems and have not been run yet.

New feature

Corvus can now install Cherry, the separate mod, and keep it up to date. Cherry has its own row on Update. The first install only ever happens when you press Download: Corvus fetches the Cherry package, checks its size and SHA-256, unpacks it into a temporary folder, checks every jar inside against the manifest — its size, SHA-256, mod id and version — and only then puts Cherry and the GeckoLib it needs into your mods folder, all together or not at all; if anything fails, the folder is put back exactly as it was. After that — and also for a Cherry you put in your mods folder yourself — new Cherry versions install automatically, even when Install updates automatically is off; that switch still governs the pack, the app and Aureum. GeckoLib is never downgraded: a copy that is already the same version or newer is kept. A package that carries a jar the manifest does not list, or another product's jar such as Aureum, is refused whole.

Installs into the mods folder now take turns: Cherry's and Aureum's installers share one lock, the Update pane never runs a pack update and a Cherry install at the same time, and quitting to apply a launcher update waits up to a minute for a running install to finish. The manifest's new cherry section is read leniently: if anything in it is wrong, Cherry is shown as not published and a line goes to the Log, while the pack and the app still update as before.

Verified by installing the real Cherry V1.0.0 package, from the manifest the website builds from that package, through this version's own code: both jars arrived byte for byte with nothing left behind, and the same manifest with one wrong digit in the package's SHA-256 was refused before anything was written. The Update pane was photographed from the built jar in English and Japanese with an installed Cherry. Not yet seen: the Download and Update buttons against the live website, and an automatic Cherry update actually running. The macOS, Windows and Linux installers for this version are built by CI on each of those systems; none of them has been run yet.

New feature

The window itself is now glass. On macOS Corvus puts AppKit's own blurred material behind a transparent window, so your desktop shows through the app the way it does through System Settings — not a picture of blur, the real thing, updating as what is behind it moves. A new Glass section on General holds two controls: Liquid Glass, which turns it on and off, and Glass tint, which sets how dark the glass is, from Clear to Dark. Turning Liquid Glass off gives the solid dark window 1.9.0 had, and that is also what Windows and Linux get: the material is a macOS API, and no attempt is made to fake it elsewhere.

The rows and cards inside the window are glass too, and much clearer than they were. Their fill was measured off a reference the owner supplied rather than chosen by eye: each card now sits 0.020 white over what is behind it, against the reference's own 0.015 to 0.054, and the shadow that used to hang under every card is gone — three of the reference's four cards have none, and a shadow is what made them read as slabs floating above the window rather than as part of it. The card border is unchanged at 0.15; the reference measures 0.149, so it was already right.

Two things that were painted on top of the glass have been removed. Three wide sky-blue fields were being drawn over the real material, which is what made the window read as droplets rather than as a pane; they now appear only when Liquid Glass is off, where they are the opaque window's atmosphere and belong. The wide blue halo behind your account picture is gone as well.

Verified by photographing the running window over a flat neutral desktop, which is the only way to tell the app's own painting from the desktop showing through: with the glass on, the window's empty field is perfectly flat and the cards sit inside the reference's own range; with it off, the atmosphere returns as designed. The macOS, Windows and Linux installers for this version were built by CI on each of those systems, but only the macOS build has been run.

New feature

Corvus now looks like macOS System Settings: a sidebar with a search field and coloured icons, and every setting laid out as rounded groups of rows. General opens with your computer account's own name and picture — on macOS read from Directory Services (verified on the developer's Mac); on Windows from the account's FullName and the AccountPicture registry entry, and on Linux from the passwd entry and ~/.face or AccountsService — those two are written to the documented locations but have NOT been run on a real Windows or Linux desktop yet, and fall back to your login name and initials if nothing can be read. Update holds the pack, the app, Aureum, and the auto-update and backup switches; Play moved to General. Nothing about how updates are checked, verified or applied changed. Verified by photographing every pane from the built jar on macOS in English, Japanese and Arabic.

New feature

Fixes Corvus never opening on macOS — it would appear in the Dock, bounce, and never show a window. 1.8.2 read your pack's mods folder, opening every jar in it, before it was allowed to draw the window; if that folder lives on an external or slow drive there was no limit on how long that took. The scan now happens in the background and the window opens straight away, with the Aureum row filling in a moment later. Verified by launching the built app with a real pack folder configured: the window appears and the scan is no longer on the drawing thread.

New feature

Stops Corvus asking you to update forever. Every 1.8.1 build reported itself as "1.8.0" no matter what it actually was, so it compared itself against the published 1.8.1, decided it was out of date, and offered an update — which installed another build that also said 1.8.0, so the offer came straight back. Taking 1.8.2 once ends the loop: 1.8.2 correctly reports 1.8.2. This affected Windows, macOS and Linux alike; macOS simply hit it first. The version now comes from the build itself rather than a number someone had to remember to edit, and the release pipeline refuses to publish a build whose reported version disagrees with the version it was built as.

New feature

Fixes the Windows installer. Until now the .msi installed Corvus but created no Start-menu entry and no desktop icon, so the install genuinely succeeded and then left you nothing to click — a progress bar would flash and vanish, which looks exactly like a failed install. The installer now creates both, shows a real setup wizard instead of running silently, and installs per-user so it needs no administrator prompt (which also means Corvus can update itself, something a Program Files install could never do). macOS and Linux are unchanged. If you have the broken 1.8.0 installed, it is most likely sitting in C:\Program Files\Corvus and can be removed from Apps & features.

New feature

Corvus can now show the changelog without leaving the app: a new Changelog page in the sidebar lists every release grouped by version, and opening one shows what changed. There is also a Website link at the bottom of the sidebar. Both follow whatever update address Corvus is set to, so a custom or mirrored address opens the matching site rather than the default one.

New feature

Corvus can now adjust some of the same settings the in-game admin app can — mob spawn rates, the Plague Doctor's natural spawn, and similar knobs — from Settings → Solo Play Tuning. This is for singleplayer only: on a multiplayer server, the server admin's own settings are what actually apply, so this screen has no effect there. It needs pack version 2.5.1 or later to work — on an older pack it will say so plainly rather than showing nothing. Changes take effect the next time you start Minecraft.

New feature

Corvus now speaks 13 languages, matching this site — Japanese, English, Spanish, French, Chinese, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Italian, Arabic, Russian, Indonesian, German and Turkish. Pick one from Settings; it takes effect the next time you open Corvus. Arabic displays right-to-left throughout. Nothing about how updating, Play or Settings works has changed.

New feature

Corvus now reads the pack's name from what this site publishes, instead of having it written into the app. Until now it always said "Glimpse Alpha" on its main screen — which became wrong the moment the pack was renamed to "Alpha", and could only have been corrected by shipping a new launcher. It now follows the name published here, so a future rename needs no launcher update at all. Nothing about updating, Play or Settings has changed. This release also rewrites several log messages that were showing raw internal detail rather than a sentence — including one that reported a failure as simply "null", which told you nothing about what had gone wrong.

New feature

Fixes a bug that could leave your Minecraft unable to start. If you installed the mod pack by hand before pointing Corvus at the folder — which the guide tells you is fine — the first update added the new mod files but could not remove the old ones, because it had no record of what was already there. Two copies of the same mod stops the game loading. Corvus now recognises the pack's own files by name, so superseded ones are removed whether or not it has a record, and it checks afterwards that only one copy of each mod is left. Your own mods are never touched: only files matching the pack's exact naming are removed, everything is backed up first, and each removal is written to the log.

New feature

Corvus can now update itself. From this version on you should not need to visit this site again: when a new Corvus is released it downloads it, checks it against the SHA-256 published here, replaces itself and reopens. It always asks first, even with automatic updates turned on — restarting the app while you are using it is not something it should decide on its own. If anything goes wrong the previous version is put back automatically: the new build has to actually start before the old one is discarded. Two honest limitations. On Linux the .deb needs administrator rights that Corvus cannot request, so it downloads and verifies the file and then shows you the exact command to run — it does not pretend to have installed it. On Windows the update reinstalls the MSI, which means Windows will show its usual permission prompt. This release also follows the site to its new address; if you are on 1.4.0 or earlier you need to download once from here by hand, because those versions are still looking for the old address and cannot be reached.

New feature

A completely rebuilt interface. Corvus now has a sidebar instead of tabs, and the Home screen shows what it actually knows at a glance — the installed version, the latest one, when it last checked, and the exact folder an update would be written into — with a live view of what it is doing underneath. Settings is a proper settings screen rather than a row of plain controls. Nothing about how updating, Play or Settings work has changed, and your existing settings carry over untouched. Two practical notes: the interface is dark only for now, and the cross-platform jar is considerably larger than before (about 10 MB rather than 1 MB) because the new interface toolkit is bundled inside it — the native installers already included it, so their size barely moved. If you use the jar and would rather not download that each time, the native installer for your system is the better option.

New feature

Fixes a bug that stopped the launcher being set up at all: if you picked your mods folder in the folder chooser — which is exactly what the chooser's own title told you to pick — it was rejected with a message that read as though your mods were missing. Either folder now works: choose your Minecraft folder or the mods folder inside it, and the launcher works out the rest and shows you the exact folder it will install into. It also now looks for your Minecraft installation on startup and offers it, so most people no longer need to go hunting for the folder at all — it always asks first and never adopts a folder on its own. One thing unchanged: a mods folder that is completely empty is still not accepted, because a folder with nothing in it cannot be told apart from an unrelated folder of the same name.

New feature

The launcher is now called Corvus, and has the Aurora Corvus logo as its app icon. Your existing settings — pack folder, automatic update preference, backup retention — are carried over the first time you run it, and the old settings folder is left in place untouched as a safety net, so nothing is lost either way. Please note that because the app's name changed, installing this version does NOT replace the old one: a new Corvus app appears alongside the existing Glimpse Launcher, which you can delete by hand once you have confirmed Corvus opens with your settings intact. The download filenames change from glimpse-launcher-* to corvus-* for the same reason. Nothing about how updating, Play, or Settings work has changed.

New feature

Fixes a serious bug in backup retention: backups were sorted by folder name, so after a version-number digit change the launcher could delete its newest backups the moment they were created while keeping older ones — exactly the backups a rollback would need. Retention now sorts by timestamp. Also fixes a case where a failed launcher self-update download could still show "Update complete" (the pack result and the launcher-download result are now reported separately and honestly). Visually, the main Update/Play decision button is now a blue glass pill in the Apple system-blue style; nothing about how updating, Play, or Settings work has changed.

New feature

Fixes a bug where the "Install updates automatically" setting on the Settings screen was saved but never actually used — turning it on didn't change anything, and clicking Update by hand was still required every time. Automatic mode now really applies a pack update on its own once it's turned on and a pack folder is set (a launcher self-update still always has to be downloaded and installed by hand, the same as before). Also reworks the whole interface's look toward a lighter, glassier style — softer, more transparent cards and rounder buttons — with no changes to how updating, Play, or Settings actually work.

New feature

Fixes a bug in 1.0.0 where clicking Update could report success without actually replacing the mod files in your pack's mods folder. Update now verifies the files it placed are really on disk before reporting success, and removes old version-stamped mod jars that a new version replaces. Also includes a redesigned Home/Settings/Log interface. If your pack folder was last updated by 1.0.0, the first update on 1.1.0 may leave one old-versioned jar behind for one cycle — see the launcher guide's "Confirming an update actually applied" section.

New feature

First release. Checks the Wiki for pack updates, verifies the download by SHA-256, backs up your mods before applying an update, and can open the official Minecraft Launcher for you. Automatic self-update is not yet included in 1.0 — check the Wiki for new launcher versions manually.
