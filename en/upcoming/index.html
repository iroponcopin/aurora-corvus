V4.2.5 — Cradle Economy and Colossus Phases: the colossus is the ring's seized motor

The update after V4.2.4. The Assembly Colossus was never a random titan — it is the Filter Ring's seized motor. You walk down from the south gate, you pay residue and condensate to hold Grace at the rim, and you strip the body for parts the viaduct and the tank can use. Timing and contents may change.

  • Pit Cut: a marked stair from the Filter Ring's south gate down to the pit rim, and a dressed Cradle Floor of dark iron-concrete plate at the bottom
  • The colossus phases rewritten: what changes between phases is how it fights, not how much health it has. The sound drops a step at each phase
  • Seizure Core: one unique drop from the body. A salvage bay and a cradle chisel break it down into parts the viaduct and the header tank can use
  • Rim Beacon: a 20-second pulse of Grace, three uses. It only works at y 56 or above, inside the ring or on the cut's stairs

V4.2.6 — Ship Freeze and QA Gate: no new place to walk

The update after V4.2.5. It adds nowhere to walk. It is the round that makes V4.2 through V4.2.5 genuinely shippable. Timing and contents may change.

  • Tag pass: every block, item and entity sits in the right tool and category tags — no more blocks that nothing can mine and no decorations the wrong tool breaks
  • Command book: one tester item that lists every /fallout verb in one place
  • Language audit: the Japanese and English key sets match exactly, with no half-written strings left anywhere
  • Regression pack and install notes: the city's maths and the pressure and core rules get measured by machine every time, and the install guide states versions, the command table and anything deliberately skipped

V4.2.7 — Hotfix on Freeze: from a frozen city to an operable one

The update after V4.2.6. V4.2.6 froze the city; this one makes the frozen city operable. Timing and contents may change.

  • A config file: turn falling debris and pipe leeches on or off without rebuilding the mod
  • A worldgen budget: the viaduct, the ring main and the underworks get a cap on work per chunk so they cannot stall chunk generation
  • A tooltip sweep: every item in this mod gains a one-line note on what it is for — the same class of gap that left nine usage texts invisible in V4.0.0
  • Crash guards on three paths: a missing dimension, a block with no data behind it, and a client-only class touched on the server

V4.3.0 — Stackworks: the city only looked industrial. Now it runs

The first update of the V4.3 line, after V4.2.7. The V4.2 line built a city that looks industrial. V4.3.0 makes it actually run. West of the Filter Ring stands the factory that used to feed the header tank and the viaduct. Ten features, no more.

  • Stackworks: a new area west of the Filter Ring, and the Cinder Spur, the route that reaches it
  • A residue line in three stages: crush, wash, press. Condensate becomes a fluid the pipes officially carry
  • Two freight stations (no train is placed for you — running one is your job) and a packaging bay that feeds them
  • A seizure gearbox (tainted, high output), a residue firebox (a dirty heat source), a mask assembly line and the ashen vault

V4.3.1 — Disaster Hotfix and Industrial Twenty: an ashen winter throws grit, not snow

The update after V4.3.0. Two workstreams in one patch. One is disaster — a season clock arrives, and blizzards come only in winter. The other is the factory — the west yard grows into a real production line. Timing and contents may change.

  • A season clock: the Ashen Conurbation gains seasons. Blizzards happen in winter only, and what falls is grit, not pretty snow
  • A main-break flood: water and condensate spill out — and they always recede. No water that rises and never leaves
  • Twenty factory additions: mostly machines, recipes and facades, with exactly one small hall added. The west yard does not become a second city
  • The disaster IDs and the factory IDs are kept apart. They arrive in one update, but they are two separate lines

V4.3.2 — Industrial Twenty and Three Chassis: the factory starts building the city's cars

  • The industrial twenty: what arrives is twenty items, not twenty machines

V4.3.3 — Garage and Fuel Discipline: the cars start obeying the blizzard, the flood and the ring

The update after V4.3.2. It makes the Rosso Runner, the Toro Wedge, the Stern Saloon, the Ashen Sparrow and the gasoline that V4.3.2 dropped into the city meet the blizzards, the floods and the Filter Ring that were already there. No new area, no boss, no fourth car — and the assembly stand becomes a garage, not a showroom. Timing and contents may change.

  • Parking Chalk and Hangar Hook: the chalk has 16 uses and marks a 3 × 5 parking pad on four set surfaces such as the Ashen Viaduct deck and Stackworks brick. A chassis parked inside a pad burns no fuel at all from 2 seconds after you get off (8 pads per player; a ninth erases the oldest). The Hangar Hook is a one-block fitting for the Stackworks yard wall or a hall ceiling support: sneak-use it empty-handed and the Ashen Sparrow hangs there, held in place, burning 1 mB every 4 seconds. One hook comes fitted next to the assembly stand, and no building is added
  • Flood and the Fuel Filter: a chassis or sparrow that sits in flood water for one second gets a contaminated tank — half speed and grit in the exhaust. A Fuel Filter (8 uses) on the vehicle clears it, at the cost of 500 mB of fuel each time. Cans in your inventory never spoil, placed gasoline caught in a flood drains away with the water, and cans can no longer be poured on the Flats, in the Vein Pit or inside the Filter Ring. Filter a wet tank and hang up a sparrow for the advancement Dry and Docked
  • Driving in a blizzard: outdoors in a winter blizzard, a chassis runs at three-quarters speed, the Ashen Sparrow climbs and dives at 60%, and the grit blinds the driver too (the same effect as on foot, never applied twice). Inside the Filter Ring vehicles still stall, and the blizzard's blindness is not brought into the ring
  • Pump tax: the factory's fuel pump gives each player the first 2000 mB a day from that pump for free. Beyond that every 1000 mB costs one stamped plate or one iron nugget (the plate goes first if you have one)
  • Traffic rules: two chassis that meet with enough speed difference bump and take 2 damage each, a chassis hitting a grounded Ashen Sparrow does 4 to the sparrow, and a flying sparrow that hits a chassis takes 3 and stops descending. Hit a train or minecart and only your vehicle takes the 8 damage and is knocked 1.5 blocks aside — the train is untouched. Touch the Assembly Colossus and you take 20 and are thrown off; the colossus is not hurt. A Pipe Leech you run over takes 4 and the vehicle none. No traffic lights, and rails remain the railway's business
  • Assembly stand, Spare Wheel and fixes: sneak-use the stand holding a wrench with a different kit in your offhand to swap kits (the old kit is destroyed, not refunded). A Spare Wheel (stacks to 8) repairs a chassis by 8, and the command /fallout park pulls your own car within 8 blocks onto the nearest pad or stand. Four V4.3.2 gaps are closed as well: the tank survives save and load, breaking an occupied stand just frees the vehicle instead of crashing, a worn-out wrench no longer silently eats a kit, and a sparrow at the world's ceiling is held there rather than kicked off

V4.3.4 — Ownership, Recipe Book and Line Card: the factory's cars get an owner

The update after V4.3.3. The three chassis and the Ashen Sparrow get an owner and can be locked. Alongside that, the factory's recipes enter the recipe book, and a Line Card lays out how the line is meant to run in eight pages. No new district and no fourth car. Timing and contents may change.

  • Ownership: whoever places a chassis or the Ashen Sparrow, or is first to mount an unowned one, becomes its owner (the action bar says so). On the assembly stand, nobody but the owner can wrench a kit off an owned vehicle. The stand itself can still be broken by anyone — no land claims. /fallout owner names the owner of the vehicle you look at within 8 blocks, and operators can clear or set it
  • Lock Collar (stacks to 16): sneak-use on a vehicle you own and it locks. Other players cannot mount it, refuel it, filter it or install a kit. They can still bump it — the rules of the road do not change. Sneak-use the collar again as owner to unlock
  • Spare Key (stacks to 16): sneak-use on your locked vehicle and one key is used up and tied to that vehicle only. Whoever holds it can mount and refuel that one vehicle as if they owned it. Break the vehicle and the key is dead
  • Recipe book: the first time you pick up a stamped plate or an industrial casing, the residue line (crush, wash, press), gasoline, the three engine kits and the avionics, the three chassis and the Ashen Sparrow, the gasoline can and the assembly stand, and this update's three items all appear in the vanilla recipe book
  • Line Card (stacks to 1): right-click opens an eight-page guide with previous and next buttons — the residue line (one crystal to two stabilised residue), condensate, gasoline and the pump tax (2000 mB a day free, then a plate or a nugget per 1000 mB), cold-starting the Ring Main from an empty tank, the Ashen Viaduct east against the Cinder Spur west, winter-only blizzards and floods that always drain, the three engine kits and the Sparrow's avionics, owners and keys, and the rules of the road against the railway and the Assembly Colossus. Crafted from paper, a survey map and a line blueprint, and found in the Stackworks hall and locker chests 40% of the time
  • Fixes: the pump-tax counter resets every 24000 city ticks (one day). Parking chalk pad counts survive a relog. Breaking a hangar hook releases the Sparrow. The fuel filter works on a Sparrow in flight, but it kills vertical movement for 10 ticks

V4.4.0 — Ashen Airfield: the sparrow finally has somewhere honest to land

The update after V4.3.4. The V4.3 line put the Ashen Sparrow in your hands with nowhere honest to land — V4.4.0 opens north, with a runway running from the Filter Ring's north gate out to the Ashen Airfield, Hangar One and a buried avgas bunker. No new dimension, no new boss, no fourth car. Timing and contents may change.

  • North Taxi: an 11-wide strip of runway deck at y 66, straight from the Filter Ring's north gate out to z=-480. Like the viaduct deck, you do not catch Residue Sickness on it. A filter lamp every 16 blocks stays dark if the main has no pressure, and the Rosso Runner, Toro Wedge and Stern Saloon can drive it — at 0.9× speed on the deck
  • Ashen Airfield: a new area at the end of the taxi, radius 80. Three windsocks stand on the cracked runway and turn with the wind. Blizzards do reach it. Spawns are kept low enough that the runway stays readable
  • Hangar One: a 36 × 12 × 24 hangar at the north end of the runway. The 3-wide door opens on redstone, or on sneak-use by a sparrow's owner within 8 blocks. Inside: a fuel pump and one chest (1–2 approach flares, a gasoline can 30% of the time, taxi chalk 20%). It is roofed, so the blizzard's grit blindness does not reach inside
  • Avgas Bunker: a gasoline tank buried under the field. It holds 32 buckets and starts with 8 already in it, so you can refuel the day you arrive. The hangar's fuel pump draws straight from any bunker within 24 blocks. Gasoline can be placed on the Ashen Airfield and in the Stackworks; in the Filter Ring, the Vein Pit and the Flats it cannot
  • Landing rules: touch the runway deck in the Ashen Sparrow at a vertical speed under 0.35 and there is no impact damage; harder than that and the airframe takes 8. An Approach Flare (4 uses), fired while flying, blocks new grit blindness for 8 seconds — the blizzard's slowdown stays
  • Taxi Chalk: chalk that only works on runway deck, drawing a 5 × 11 stand (16 uses). A sparrow or chassis fully on the stand burns no gasoline while parked. Also two advancements, Land the Sparrow and Hangar One, and the command /fallout field

V4.4.1 — Hangar Plumbing and Approach Polish: the hangar pump starts drinking the bunker

The update after V4.4.0. V4.4.0 poured the runway strip and the hangar box; V4.4.1 makes them behave — Hangar One and the Avgas Bunker on the Ashen Airfield get real plumbing, and the North Taxi gets lights you can read in grit. No new area, chassis or airframe, and no second airfield. Timing and contents may change.

  • Bunker Hatch and shaft: the makeshift trapdoor over the Avgas Bunker becomes a proper Bunker Hatch, with a 2 × 2 shaft and a ladder down to the tank at y 58. Leave the hatch broken and the bunker bleeds 20 mB a second, gasoline drips, and anyone nearby is told "Bunker leaking. Fit the hatch."
  • The pump drinks the bunker: every can or chassis you fill at the Hangar One pump now really lowers the Avgas Bunker — there is no bottomless hidden buffer. It holds 32,000 mB (32 buckets), new worlds start at 8,000, and worlds that already exist keep whatever they have; the update does not refill them. The command /fallout bunker shows the level and whether the pump is linked (within 24 blocks of the bunker)
  • Approach Lights: cyan lamps on both shoulders of the North Taxi (x ±5), one every 8 blocks from z=−136 to z=−472. They light while the bunker holds at least 1,000 mB or the Ring Main is pressurised — either source is enough. Unlit, they are a dull dark grey with no glow
  • Approach Flare at 12 seconds, stubs recycled: the flare lasts 12 seconds instead of 8 (configurable 6–20). A spent flare leaves a Flare Stub in your inventory, and 2 stubs plus 1 iron nugget always make 1 flare — no dice roll. The flare still only stops fresh grit-blindness; the blizzard slow stays
  • A landing table: touch the Runway Deck in the Ashen Sparrow below 0.25 vertical speed for no damage and "Soft on the taxi.", 0.25–0.49 for 4 damage, 0.50 or more for 8 damage and a one-second stall. It replaces V4.4.0's single 0.35 / 8 threshold
  • Door, hook and chalk fixes: the Hangar Door remembers whether it is open, and a chunk unloading mid-roll snaps it to where you left it (toggles wait 0.75 seconds and never double-sound). A sparrow on a Hangar Hook or on the hangar floor counts as roofed, so grit never reaches it. Taxi Chalk pads survive relogging like Parking Chalk pads (8 per player). Breaking the hatch above someone in the shaft does not suffocate them. No second airfield

V4.4.2 — Crosswind and Bunker Lock: the wind pushes, and the hatch obeys its owner

The update after V4.4.1. Wind starts blowing across the Ashen Airfield and pushes any Ashen Sparrow or chassis left loose on the North Taxi. The Avgas Bunker and the Hangar One door can be closed to strangers with a Lock Collar. Still one field — no new area, chassis or airframe. Timing and contents may change.

  • Field wind: the Ashen Airfield gets a wind with a heading and a strength. Strength runs 0.05–0.35, with 0.15 added during a winter blizzard (capped at 0.50). The heading eases toward a new one every 2–4 minutes, and the windsocks swing to one of 8 headings to show it. It does not blow inside Hangar One, inside the Filter Ring, or below y 40
  • Crosswind drift: an Ashen Sparrow left loose on the taxi or the field drifts at 12% of the wind, a chassis at 5%. A frame on a hangar hook, on a taxi-chalk pad or under a chock does not move. The wind never adds landing damage on its own
  • Windsock Ribbon: hold it and the wind's heading and strength appear on the action bar (Wind 240° 0.22). 16 uses. The command /fallout wind prints the same numbers
  • Locks for the hatch and the door: the first player to sneak-use a Lock Collar on the Bunker Hatch owns the Avgas Bunker. Once locked, others can neither open the hatch nor draw from the hangar pump tied to that bunker (a Spare Key lets one person through). The Hangar One door locks the same way, and only the owner can toggle it. Redstone still moves it, on purpose — this is a lock, not a land claim. Pumping alone never claims a bunker, and an unowned one stays public
  • Chock: use it on a Sparrow or chassis sitting on the Runway Deck or the hangar floor and it stays put — no drift and no idle fuel drain. The owner removes it by sneak-using an empty hand, and the chock comes back. You cannot chock someone else's locked frame
  • Hotfixes: two Sparrows on the same taxi cell bump for 2 damage instead of clipping into each other. Approach lights only go dark when the bunker is under 1000 mB and the Filter Ring main is uncharged at the same time. The leak warning gets a per-player cooldown, and strangers cannot read a locked bunker's level by command

V4.5.0 — Dispatch Loft and Grid Loop: the four-armed city finally gets a brain

The update after V4.4.2. The city had four arms and no brain. A Dispatch Loft goes on top of the plaza monument in the Filter Ring, and a radio and a set of relays tie together the four hubs that already exist. No new land, no fifth road. Timing and contents may change.

  • Dispatch Loft: a 9 × 9 room at y 71 on top of the plaza monument in the Filter Ring, with a 3 × 3 hole and a ladder down to the plaza. Inside: one Dispatch Board, one Grid Relay, two filter lamps (they follow the ring main's pressure) and one chest (a Grid Radio 40% of the time, and 1–3 Freight Waybills). The survey map gains a loft mark. It is not a new area — it is still part of the Filter Ring
  • Dispatch Board: a block that rewrites the state of the city every 2 seconds — ring pressure and header tank level, sump level, avgas level, wind heading and strength, whether the Assembly Colossus is alive or slain, and the season. A tank that is not there reads ---
  • Grid Radio: a four-channel handset. Right-click cycles the channel (ring and sump / avgas and wind / whether the east and west freight anchors are in place / colossus and season); sneak-use sends that channel's one-liner to everyone in the Ashen Conurbation, 4 seconds between pings. Outside the Ashen Conurbation it says the radio is dead here and does nothing. Two advancements (Climb the Loft, Four Arms)
  • Grid Loop: Grid Relays at seven places that already exist — the Bleed Shed in the east, the Stackworks station in the west, the pit rim in the south, the end of the North Taxi, the Sump Hall header tank, the avgas bunker and the ring itself. New pressure main is limited to jogs of 16 blocks or less that tie a relay to a nearby main or tank; a relay that cannot be reached is marked isolated, still answers the radio and moves no fluid. Not one new road
  • Fluid on the grid: if the condensate relays link up, up to 50 mB a second moves from the fuller header tank to the emptier one, between the Sump Hall and the tank V4.3.0 left empty in Stackworks (paths of 64 nodes or less). Gasoline stays on the bunker and Hangar One side, never shares a relay with condensate, and not one drop enters the Filter Ring
  • Freight Waybill: a slip marked with a destination (east, west, south, north or loft), stacking to 16. Use it at a freight anchor or a signal-only relay: the right hub gives Speed I for 10 seconds, the wrong one tells you the destination's coordinates. The Freight Ticket stays. This is still not a moving train — it is signage

Planned No date yet

V4.5.1 — Radio Discipline and Relay Honesty: the radio gets a price, and a relay admits when it is cut off

The update after V4.5.0. The Dispatch Loft and the seven Grid Relays that V4.5.0 placed stay as they are; this one puts a price on the radio and makes each relay show its own state. No new area, no new vehicle, no eighth relay. Timing and contents may change.

  • Radio Cell: a Grid Radio ping now needs a Radio Cell in your off-hand or inventory. Each ping spends 1 of the cell's 64 uses; switching channels stays free. The Dispatch Loft chest holds 1–2 cells half the time
  • Radio manners: the 4-second cooldown stays, and a ping inside it only says the radio is cooling and spends nothing. The radio remembers its channel, and a ping reaches only players who are still in the Ashen Conurbation — anyone who left the city mid-ping does not get it
  • Relay Probe: sneak-use it on a Grid Relay and it reads out the relay's mode, whether it is isolated, and how many same-mode relays it reaches along the pressure mains (counted up to 64 nodes). It is made from a Sump Gauge (or a factory gauge) and one Grid Relay. Reading an isolated or live relay earns the advancement Probe the Grid
  • Relay looks and the command /fallout relay: a Grid Relay now shows its own state — a dark core when isolated, a cyan flash for condensate, an amber flash for gasoline, grey for signal. The V4.2.4 mains are not recoloured. Look at a relay within 8 blocks and /fallout relay prints the same line the probe does
  • Fixed modes: the Sump Hall and Filter Ring relays are locked to condensate and the avgas bunker relay to gasoline; an operator who tries to change them is told that hub forces its mode. The condensate path can no longer pull avgas into the header tank, an isolated relay never starts a 50 mB move, and with the grid switched off nothing moves at all
  • Dispatch Board fixes: the board now gives a comparator signal — 0 while the Filter Ring is unpressurised, 8 when it is pressurised and the header tank holds at least 200 mB, 15 when avgas is at 1000 mB or more on top of that. The board keeps its last reading, so for the first 2 seconds after a chunk reloads it may show the old numbers before going live. A Freight Waybill for the loft is now accepted at the loft's relay or at the board, and the loft lamps follow the V4.2.4 pressure rules instead of staying lit

Timing and contents can change, and anything here may be dropped. Only what actually ships appears in the changelog.
