Coming next

Nothing on this page is in your game yet. These are the things being built, and the things expected in the next release, told before they are settled.

V4.4.1 — Hangar Plumbing and Approach Polish: the hangar pump starts drinking the bunker

The update after V4.4.0. V4.4.0 poured the runway strip and the hangar box; V4.4.1 makes them behave — Hangar One and the Avgas Bunker on the Ashen Airfield get real plumbing, and the North Taxi gets lights you can read in grit. No new area, chassis or airframe, and no second airfield. Timing and contents may change.

  • Bunker Hatch and shaft: the makeshift trapdoor over the Avgas Bunker becomes a proper Bunker Hatch, with a 2 × 2 shaft and a ladder down to the tank at y 58. Leave the hatch broken and the bunker bleeds 20 mB a second, gasoline drips, and anyone nearby is told "Bunker leaking. Fit the hatch."
  • The pump drinks the bunker: every can or chassis you fill at the Hangar One pump now really lowers the Avgas Bunker — there is no bottomless hidden buffer. It holds 32,000 mB (32 buckets), new worlds start at 8,000, and worlds that already exist keep whatever they have; the update does not refill them. The command /fallout bunker shows the level and whether the pump is linked (within 24 blocks of the bunker)
  • Approach Lights: cyan lamps on both shoulders of the North Taxi (x ±5), one every 8 blocks from z=−136 to z=−472. They light while the bunker holds at least 1,000 mB or the Ring Main is pressurised — either source is enough. Unlit, they are a dull dark grey with no glow
  • Approach Flare at 12 seconds, stubs recycled: the flare lasts 12 seconds instead of 8 (configurable 6–20). A spent flare leaves a Flare Stub in your inventory, and 2 stubs plus 1 iron nugget always make 1 flare — no dice roll. The flare still only stops fresh grit-blindness; the blizzard slow stays
  • A landing table: touch the Runway Deck in the Ashen Sparrow below 0.25 vertical speed for no damage and "Soft on the taxi.", 0.25–0.49 for 4 damage, 0.50 or more for 8 damage and a one-second stall. It replaces V4.4.0's single 0.35 / 8 threshold
  • Door, hook and chalk fixes: the Hangar Door remembers whether it is open, and a chunk unloading mid-roll snaps it to where you left it (toggles wait 0.75 seconds and never double-sound). A sparrow on a Hangar Hook or on the hangar floor counts as roofed, so grit never reaches it. Taxi Chalk pads survive relogging like Parking Chalk pads (8 per player). Breaking the hatch above someone in the shaft does not suffocate them. No second airfield

V4.4.2 — Crosswind and Bunker Lock: the wind pushes, and the hatch obeys its owner

The update after V4.4.1. Wind starts blowing across the Ashen Airfield and pushes any Ashen Sparrow or chassis left loose on the North Taxi. The Avgas Bunker and the Hangar One door can be closed to strangers with a Lock Collar. Still one field — no new area, chassis or airframe. Timing and contents may change.

  • Field wind: the Ashen Airfield gets a wind with a heading and a strength. Strength runs 0.05–0.35, with 0.15 added during a winter blizzard (capped at 0.50). The heading eases toward a new one every 2–4 minutes, and the windsocks swing to one of 8 headings to show it. It does not blow inside Hangar One, inside the Filter Ring, or below y 40
  • Crosswind drift: an Ashen Sparrow left loose on the taxi or the field drifts at 12% of the wind, a chassis at 5%. A frame on a hangar hook, on a taxi-chalk pad or under a chock does not move. The wind never adds landing damage on its own
  • Windsock Ribbon: hold it and the wind's heading and strength appear on the action bar (Wind 240° 0.22). 16 uses. The command /fallout wind prints the same numbers
  • Locks for the hatch and the door: the first player to sneak-use a Lock Collar on the Bunker Hatch owns the Avgas Bunker. Once locked, others can neither open the hatch nor draw from the hangar pump tied to that bunker (a Spare Key lets one person through). The Hangar One door locks the same way, and only the owner can toggle it. Redstone still moves it, on purpose — this is a lock, not a land claim. Pumping alone never claims a bunker, and an unowned one stays public
  • Chock: use it on a Sparrow or chassis sitting on the Runway Deck or the hangar floor and it stays put — no drift and no idle fuel drain. The owner removes it by sneak-using an empty hand, and the chock comes back. You cannot chock someone else's locked frame
  • Hotfixes: two Sparrows on the same taxi cell bump for 2 damage instead of clipping into each other. Approach lights only go dark when the bunker is under 1000 mB and the Filter Ring main is uncharged at the same time. The leak warning gets a per-player cooldown, and strangers cannot read a locked bunker's level by command

V4.5.0 — Dispatch Loft and Grid Loop: the four-armed city finally gets a brain

The update after V4.4.2. The city had four arms and no brain. A Dispatch Loft goes on top of the plaza monument in the Filter Ring, and a radio and a set of relays tie together the four hubs that already exist. No new land, no fifth road. Timing and contents may change.

  • Dispatch Loft: a 9 × 9 room at y 71 on top of the plaza monument in the Filter Ring, with a 3 × 3 hole and a ladder down to the plaza. Inside: one Dispatch Board, one Grid Relay, two filter lamps (they follow the ring main's pressure) and one chest (a Grid Radio 40% of the time, and 1–3 Freight Waybills). The survey map gains a loft mark. It is not a new area — it is still part of the Filter Ring
  • Dispatch Board: a block that rewrites the state of the city every 2 seconds — ring pressure and header tank level, sump level, avgas level, wind heading and strength, whether the Assembly Colossus is alive or slain, and the season. A tank that is not there reads ---
  • Grid Radio: a four-channel handset. Right-click cycles the channel (ring and sump / avgas and wind / whether the east and west freight anchors are in place / colossus and season); sneak-use sends that channel's one-liner to everyone in the Ashen Conurbation, 4 seconds between pings. Outside the Ashen Conurbation it says the radio is dead here and does nothing. Two advancements (Climb the Loft, Four Arms)
  • Grid Loop: Grid Relays at seven places that already exist — the Bleed Shed in the east, the Stackworks station in the west, the pit rim in the south, the end of the North Taxi, the Sump Hall header tank, the avgas bunker and the ring itself. New pressure main is limited to jogs of 16 blocks or less that tie a relay to a nearby main or tank; a relay that cannot be reached is marked isolated, still answers the radio and moves no fluid. Not one new road
  • Fluid on the grid: if the condensate relays link up, up to 50 mB a second moves from the fuller header tank to the emptier one, between the Sump Hall and the tank V4.3.0 left empty in Stackworks (paths of 64 nodes or less). Gasoline stays on the bunker and Hangar One side, never shares a relay with condensate, and not one drop enters the Filter Ring
  • Freight Waybill: a slip marked with a destination (east, west, south, north or loft), stacking to 16. Use it at a freight anchor or a signal-only relay: the right hub gives Speed I for 10 seconds, the wrong one tells you the destination's coordinates. The Freight Ticket stays. This is still not a moving train — it is signage

V4.5.1 — Radio Discipline and Relay Honesty: the radio gets a price, and a relay admits when it is cut off

The update after V4.5.0. The Dispatch Loft and the seven Grid Relays that V4.5.0 placed stay as they are; this one puts a price on the radio and makes each relay show its own state. No new area, no new vehicle, no eighth relay. Timing and contents may change.

  • Radio Cell: a Grid Radio ping now needs a Radio Cell in your off-hand or inventory. Each ping spends 1 of the cell's 64 uses; switching channels stays free. The Dispatch Loft chest holds 1–2 cells half the time
  • Radio manners: the 4-second cooldown stays, and a ping inside it only says the radio is cooling and spends nothing. The radio remembers its channel, and a ping reaches only players who are still in the Ashen Conurbation — anyone who left the city mid-ping does not get it
  • Relay Probe: sneak-use it on a Grid Relay and it reads out the relay's mode, whether it is isolated, and how many same-mode relays it reaches along the pressure mains (counted up to 64 nodes). It is made from a Sump Gauge (or a factory gauge) and one Grid Relay. Reading an isolated or live relay earns the advancement Probe the Grid
  • Relay looks and the command /fallout relay: a Grid Relay now shows its own state — a dark core when isolated, a cyan flash for condensate, an amber flash for gasoline, grey for signal. The V4.2.4 mains are not recoloured. Look at a relay within 8 blocks and /fallout relay prints the same line the probe does
  • Fixed modes: the Sump Hall and Filter Ring relays are locked to condensate and the avgas bunker relay to gasoline; an operator who tries to change them is told that hub forces its mode. The condensate path can no longer pull avgas into the header tank, an isolated relay never starts a 50 mB move, and with the grid switched off nothing moves at all
  • Dispatch Board fixes: the board now gives a comparator signal — 0 while the Filter Ring is unpressurised, 8 when it is pressurised and the header tank holds at least 200 mB, 15 when avgas is at 1000 mB or more on top of that. The board keeps its last reading, so for the first 2 seconds after a chunk reloads it may show the old numbers before going live. A Freight Waybill for the loft is now accepted at the loft's relay or at the board, and the loft lamps follow the V4.2.4 pressure rules instead of staying lit

Timing and contents can change, and anything here may be dropped. Only what actually ships appears in the changelog.
